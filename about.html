    PhD in Particle Physics

    Universidad de Cantabria, Santander, Spain
    (09.2019 – 03.2025)
    Under the supervision of Luca Scodellaro, I successfully presented my thesis:
    "Search For Supersymmetric Particle Pair Production In Final States With Two Oppositely Charged Leptons And Large Missing Transverse Momentum In Proton-proton Collisions At 13 TeV"

    This thesis was focused on probing whether the data recorded at the CMS detector showed any evidence of new particles beyond the ones observed (known as the Standard Model). In particular, the Supersymmetric model was tested, given its multiple advantages in answering the open questions in particle physics. While no evidence of the existence of these particles was found, the stringest constraints to date were placed, thus helping reject many of the possible Supersymmetric models.

    Whilst the goal of the PhD was scientifically motivated, its consecution involved applying and developing several data analysis techniques on very large datasets, using AI tools, Monte Carlo simulations and distributed computing with numerous parallel jobs on a large cluster.

    M.Sc. in Particle Physics

    I specialised in a masters in particle physics, although I not only learnt about particle physics but also several machine learning tools. I spent one year working on my master thesis, titled "Higgs production cross section at 13 TeV and prospects on BSM searches for the HL-LHC".
